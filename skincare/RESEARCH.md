# Skincare — Evidence-Based Reference

A working reference compiled from systematic reviews, meta-analyses, RCTs and
clinical guidelines. Each section rates how strong the evidence is, so marketing
claims can be told apart from what has been shown in trials.

> Not medical advice. Anything that persists, spreads, bleeds, changes shape or
> colour, or hurts should be seen by a GP or dermatologist.

**Evidence grades used below**

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Multiple RCTs / systematic review or meta-analysis / formal guideline |
| **B** | Some RCTs, or consistent smaller trials, often industry-funded |
| **C** | Mechanistic data, narrative reviews, expert opinion |
| **D** | Mostly marketing / anecdote |

---

## 1. How skin works (the minimum you need)

- **Stratum corneum (barrier):** the dead outer layer, built like "bricks and
  mortar". The "mortar" is a lipid mix of ceramides, cholesterol and free fatty
  acids. A damaged barrier lets more water escape (measured as *trans-epidermal
  water loss*, TEWL) and lets irritants in, so the skin gets redness, flaking and
  stinging.
- **Epidermis:** turns over roughly every 4 weeks. Melanocytes here make pigment.
- **Dermis:** collagen and elastin. UV breaks these down, causing wrinkles, loss
  of elasticity and enlarged-looking pores.
- **Pilosebaceous unit:** the hair follicle plus its oil gland. This is where
  acne, seborrhoeic dermatitis (via *Malassezia* yeast feeding on sebum), and
  razor bumps start.
- **Sebum** is driven mainly by androgens (testosterone/DHT). Men typically have
  higher output, which matters for pores, shine and seborrhoeic dermatitis.

---

## 2. The evidence hierarchy of skincare (most to least proven)

1. **Sun protection** (A)
2. **Topical retinoids** (A)
3. **Not damaging the barrier** (gentle cleansing + moisturiser) (A/B)
4. **Treating any specific condition with its proven drug** (acne, rosacea,
   seborrhoeic dermatitis) (A)
5. Supporting actives: azelaic acid (A for rosacea/acne), niacinamide (B),
   vitamin C (B), AHA/BHA exfoliants (B/C)
6. Almost everything else (peptides, "growth factors", botanicals, collagen
   creams) (C/D)

---

## 3. Sunscreen — the single best-proven intervention

**Key trial:** Hughes et al., *Annals of Internal Medicine* 2013 (RCT, n=903,
Australia). Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen for 4.5 years gave **no detectable
increase in skin ageing**, with **24% less ageing** than discretionary use
(relative odds 0.76, 95% CI 0.59–0.98). β-carotene supplements had no effect.
Grade **A**.

**Practical evidence:**
- SPF is tested at **2 mg/cm²**. Real people apply 0.5–1.5 mg/cm², and protection
  falls steeply as the amount drops. For face plus neck that is roughly
  **½ teaspoon**, or about two finger-lengths for the face alone.
- Reapply when you're outdoors for long periods (about every 2 hours, and after
  sweating or swimming).
- **Visible light** (not just UV) drives pigmentation, especially melasma and
  post-inflammatory marks. **Tinted sunscreens containing iron oxides** protect
  against visible light. RCTs show better melasma outcomes than UV-only sunscreen
  (one cited study: 75% vs 60% MASI reduction). Grade **B**, and many studies were
  industry-sponsored.
- Use SPF 30–50, broad spectrum (UVA + UVB), every morning. Pick the formula you'll
  actually wear daily; adherence beats small differences in filters.

## 4. Retinoids — the best-proven "active"

**Photoageing:**
- Systematic review of 7 RCTs (Sitohang 2022): tretinoin improved wrinkling,
  mottled pigmentation, sallowness and lentigines from about 1 month, and the
  effect lasted to 24 months. **A**
- Meta-analysis (8 vehicle-controlled trials, n=1,361): significant improvement
  in fine *and* coarse wrinkles. **A**
- Network meta-analysis 2025 (23 RCTs, n=3,905): isotretinoin, **retinol** and
  tretinoin all significantly improved fine wrinkles.
- Comparative SR 2024 (25 studies): many comparators were equivalent to tretinoin
  and **better tolerated**. Irritation is tretinoin's main drawback.

**Acne:** the AAD 2024 guideline gives a **strong recommendation** for topical
retinoids (adapalene, tretinoin, tazarotene, trifarotene). Adapalene is the most
tolerable and is available over the counter in many countries (0.1%). **A**

**Types, weakest to strongest:** retinyl esters → retinol → retinal (retinaldehyde)
→ adapalene → tretinoin → tazarotene.

**How to use (to limit "retinoid dermatitis"):**
- At night, apply a **pea-sized amount for the whole face** to dry skin.
- Start 2–3 nights a week and build up over 4–8 weeks.
- "Sandwich" with moisturiser if you're dry or irritated.
- Expect some dryness or peeling in weeks 2–6. Results take **3–6 months** for
  ageing and about 12 weeks for acne.
- Avoid in pregnancy or when trying to conceive (tretinoin/tazarotene).
- Keep it away from the eye corners, nostril creases and lips.

## 5. Cleansing and moisturising (barrier care)

- **Cleanser:** a gentle, low-pH or syndet (synthetic detergent) cleanser.
  Harsh soaps raise skin pH and strip lipids. Once or twice a day is enough.
  Lukewarm water, no scrubbing.
- **Moisturiser ingredient types:**
  - *Humectants* (glycerin, hyaluronic acid, urea) draw water in.
  - *Occlusives* (petrolatum, dimethicone, lanolin) stop water escaping.
  - *Emollients/lipids* (ceramides, fatty acids) fill gaps in the barrier.
- **Ceramides:** a 2023 meta-analysis (atopic dermatitis) found ceramide
  moisturisers improved clinical severity (SCORAD) more than other moisturisers,
  but **not TEWL**. Good rationale, but no clear edge over a well-formulated
  ordinary moisturiser. **B**
- Moisturiser is what makes retinoids, acids and acne treatments tolerable.

## 6. Supporting actives

### Azelaic acid (15–20%)
- 2023 systematic review (43 RCTs): **better than placebo for rosacea, acne and
  melasma**. Rosacea has the strongest evidence. **A**
- Anti-inflammatory, antibacterial and anti-pigment. Safe in pregnancy.
- Mild stinging at first is common and usually fades within weeks. A good
  "first active" for redness-prone skin.

### Niacinamide (2–5%)
- **Pigmentation:** an RCT of 4% niacinamide against 4% hydroquinone for melasma
  found comparable results with fewer side effects (Navarrete-Solís 2011). **B**
- **Sebum:** 2% reduced facial sebum (Draelos 2006, small study). **B/C**
- **Barrier:** increases ceramide synthesis, and a niacinamide moisturiser helped
  barrier function in rosacea. **B**
- **Overall appearance:** split-face RCT, 5% for 12 weeks, improved fine lines,
  spots, texture, redness and sallowness. **B** (industry-funded)
- Well tolerated. Concentrations above 10% add irritation, not proven benefit.

### Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid)
- Systematic review (44 studies): L-ascorbic acid is the most clinically
  validated form. Low pH (<3.5) with vitamin E and ferulic acid improves
  photoageing. **B**
- Unstable (turns orange or brown when oxidised). Derivatives are more stable
  but less proven.
- Best used in the morning **under** sunscreen as an antioxidant. It doesn't
  replace SPF.

### Exfoliating acids
- **Salicylic acid (BHA, 0.5–2%)** is oil-soluble and clears blocked pores.
  Useful for blackheads, oily skin and acne. **B/C**
- **Glycolic or lactic acid (AHAs)** smooth texture and help pigment. The
  evidence is mostly from in-clinic peels. **C**
- Overuse damages the barrier. 2–3 times a week is plenty, and you need less
  alongside a retinoid.

### Benzoyl peroxide (2.5–5%)
- AAD 2024: **strong recommendation** for acne. It kills *C. acnes* and doesn't
  cause bacterial resistance. Lower concentrations irritate less and work about
  as well. It bleaches fabric. **A**

## 7. Conditions relevant to adult male facial skin

### Acne
- First line: **adapalene + benzoyl peroxide** (AAD 2024, strong). Add azelaic
  acid if needed. Persistent, scarring or nodular acne → see a doctor (oral
  options including isotretinoin).

### Rosacea
- Look for central-face redness (cheeks, nose, chin), flushing, papules/pustules,
  visible vessels and stinging.
- The **ROSCO 2019/2020** consensus treats each *feature* (phenotype) separately:
  - persistent redness → brimonidine or oxymetazoline (Rx)
  - papules → **ivermectin 1%**, azelaic acid or metronidazole (Rx)
  - vessels → vascular laser/IPL
- Triggers: heat, alcohol, spicy food, UV, harsh products.
- Gentle care and daily sunscreen underpin every treatment.

### Seborrhoeic dermatitis
- Red, greasy-flaky patches at the **eyebrows, between the brows, the sides of
  the nose (nasolabial folds), the hairline, behind the ears and in the beard**.
  Driven by *Malassezia* yeast plus sebum.
- **Cochrane review:** ketoconazole cream/gel beats placebo, works about as well
  as steroids with fewer side effects, and ciclopirox works well on the face. **A**
- Practical: **ketoconazole 2% shampoo** used as a face/beard wash, left on for
  3–5 minutes, 2–3 times a week. Flares are common, so expect ongoing maintenance.
- Beards can make it worse, since they trap sebum and yeast. Wash and condition
  the beard itself.

### Pseudofolliculitis barbae / razor bumps / shaving irritation
- Shaved, curly hairs curve back into the skin and cause inflammation.
- Evidence (Gray & McMichael 2017) is weak for "single-blade only". Pre-shave
  hydration and post-shave moisturiser help. Leaving about 0.5 mm of stubble
  (clippers, electric trimmer) and shaving *with* the grain are the standard
  advice. **C**
- Topicals: retinoid, low-strength BHA or glycolic acid, benzoyl peroxide if
  pustular. Laser hair removal for severe cases.

### Enlarged pores
- Causes: high sebum, follicle size (genetic), and loss of elasticity from
  photoageing. Reviews admit the evidence is limited and there's no standard way
  to measure pores.
- Most rational: **retinoid** (collagen + oil), **BHA**, **niacinamide**
  (sebum), **sunscreen** (keeps the walls supported). Lasers and devices are the
  most robust options. Pores can't be "closed" permanently.

### Under-eye dark circles / periorbital hyperpigmentation
- Several causes, often in the same person: pigment, **visible vessels through
  thin skin** (bluish or purple), **shadowing from the tear trough** (structural),
  puffiness, rubbing from allergy or eczema, and lack of sleep.
- No gold-standard treatment. Pigment types respond somewhat to retinoids,
  vitamin C, niacinamide, azelaic acid and sunscreen. Vascular and structural
  types need procedures (filler, laser) or just camouflage. **C**

### Post-inflammatory erythema/hyperpigmentation (marks after spots or irritation)
- Red marks (PIE) fade over months. Vascular laser speeds this up.
- Brown marks (PIH): **sunscreen (tinted)**, azelaic acid, niacinamide,
  retinoids, vitamin C.
- Don't pick.

### Chapped lips
- Use an occlusive balm (petrolatum). Avoid licking and fragrance/menthol balms.
  Persistent scaling at the corners → angular cheilitis (yeast or bacteria), see
  a doctor.

### Moles and spots
- Monitor with the **ABCDE** rule: Asymmetry, Border, Colour variation,
  Diameter >6 mm, Evolution. Any change or new lump that grows or bleeds should
  be checked by a professional.

## 8. Lifestyle (honest grading)

| Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Smoking → premature ageing | **A** (twin and cohort studies) |
| High-glycaemic diet → acne | **Mixed.** 2022 SR (34 studies): modest association, with some RCT support. 2025 meta-analysis (5 studies): no significant pooled link. **B/C** |
| Dairy → acne | **Mixed**, may depend on the population. **C** |
| Poor sleep → skin ageing, slower barrier recovery | One small, industry-funded study (2013). Plausible but unconfirmed. **C** |
| Drinking water → skin hydration | Little evidence beyond normal hydration. **D** |
| Oral collagen supplements | Some small, mostly industry RCTs. **C** |

## 9. Myths and common mistakes

- **"More products = better."** Each extra active raises the risk of irritation.
- **Mixing myths:** niacinamide + vitamin C is fine in modern formulations.
  Retinoid + benzoyl peroxide is fine with adapalene (a fixed combination
  exists). Older tretinoin is degraded by benzoyl peroxide, so use them at
  different times of day.
- **"Natural" ≠ safe.** Essential oils and fragrance are among the commonest
  contact allergens.
- **Tingling ≠ working.** Stinging usually means barrier irritation.
- **Results need time:** 6–12 weeks for acne, 3–6 months for ageing or
  pigmentation. Change one thing at a time so you can tell what's helping.

## 10. Evidence-based baseline routine (adult male, normal–oily skin)

| | Step | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| AM | Gentle cleanse (or water rinse) | Use a ketoconazole wash on affected areas 2–3×/week if seborrhoeic dermatitis is present |
| AM | (Optional) Vitamin C or niacinamide | |
| AM | Moisturiser | Can skip if your sunscreen is moisturising |
| AM | **Sunscreen SPF 30–50, ½ tsp face+neck** | Tinted with iron oxide if you have pigment concerns |
| PM | Gentle cleanse | |
| PM | **Retinoid** (adapalene 0.1% or retinol → tretinoin) | Start 2–3 nights a week |
| PM | Moisturiser | |
| Targeted | Azelaic acid (redness/marks), BHA (pores/blackheads), BPO (spots) | Add one at a time |

Then adapt it to the specific findings from the photo analysis.
