## The unifying pattern

The redness clusters in the **eyebrows, between the brows, the sides of the nose
and the nasolabial folds**, together with **follicular spots on the chest**.
That combination is the textbook distribution of *Malassezia*-driven conditions
(seborrhoeic dermatitis ± Malassezia folliculitis). The central nose and cheek
redness could also be **rosacea**. The two often overlap.

## Questions that sharpen this

1. Does the nose and cheek redness stay when you haven't just showered, or does

   it's more likely seborrhoeic dermatitis or rosacea.)
2. Any flaking or dandruff in the brows, the scalp, beside the nose or in the beard?
3. Do you flush with heat, alcohol, spicy food or exercise? Any stinging when

4. Is the chest itchy? Is it worse with sweat or heat?
5. What are you currently using on your face (soap, shower gel, nothing)?

## Plan

### Phase 1 (weeks 0–4): calm and treat the yeast/redness
- **AM:** rinse or gentle cleanser → moisturiser → **SPF 30–50 every day**
  (½ tsp face + neck).
- **Ketoconazole 2% shampoo** (e.g. Nizoral) as a *mask*, **3×/week**, on the
  brows, between the brows, the sides of the nose, the beard and the upper chest.
  Lather, leave **3–5 min**, rinse. Reduce to 1×/week for maintenance once clear.
- **PM:** gentle cleanser → fragrance-free moisturiser (look for glycerin, ceramides
  or niacinamide).
- **Lips:** plain petrolatum (Vaseline/Aquaphor) several times a day and at night.
  Stop licking them.
- **Avoid for now:** scrubs, strong acids, fragrance, menthol, alcohol toners, and
  very hot water on the face.

### Phase 2 (weeks 4–12): add one active
- **Azelaic acid 10–20%**, once daily. It is evidence-graded A for rosacea
  redness and bumps, helps marks and pores, and is gentle. Mild tingling for the
  first week or two is normal.
- (Or niacinamide 4–5% if azelaic acid stings too much.)

### Phase 3 (month 3+): long-term
- Once the redness is controlled, add a **retinoid** at night (adapalene 0.1% or
  retinol, 2–3 nights a week) for pores and long-term ageing.
- Chest: if it's still spotty, use **benzoyl peroxide 4–5% wash** (left 1–2 min)
  on alternate days with ketoconazole.

## See a doctor / dermatologist if
- the redness persists after 4–6 weeks of the above (for prescription ivermectin,
  metronidazole, a short mild steroid, or a vascular laser);
- the nose develops visible vessels, thickening, or pus bumps;
- the chest spots spread or itch a lot;
- the neck mole changes (Asymmetry, Border, Colour, Diameter, Evolution).

## Tracking
Re-photograph in the **same spot, same light, before showering** every 2–4 weeks.
